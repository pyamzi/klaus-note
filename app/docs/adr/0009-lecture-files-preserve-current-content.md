# Lecture files preserve current content independently of edit history

Accepted in the lecture design interview on 2026-10-06. A Lecture's folder must contain everything needed to restore its current content without a Klaus account or a separate edit history. The original Source deck stays unaltered; the timestamped Transcript remains Markdown, with lecture notes, relationships and personal annotations preserved in portable files. A generated A4 study PDF presents that content, including visible summaries and transcript text in PDF sticky-note annotations as requested in the follow-up design review. These notes supplement the canonical Markdown transcript; they do not remove it. History may additionally support undo and collaboration, but is not required to recover the Lecture's current state.

This amends [ADR-0002](0002-materials-are-plain-pdf-and-markdown.md) and [ADR-0005](0005-edit-history-is-truth-google-docs-model.md) for Lectures only. Keeping every contribution solely inside one mutable PDF or solely in authoritative application history conflicts with the accepted requirement to preserve pristine slides and recover the lecture from its folder. General Materials retain their existing policy.

A study PDF that acquires personal annotations in KlausNote or an external editor contains new user data. It must be preserved until that work is durably incorporated into the lecture files; it cannot simply be deleted as a reproducible export. Annotated sheet layouts remain stable when generated explanations grow; additional content uses continuation pages. The exact annotation representation and import/merge rules remain open in the [design interview](../research/lecture-study-interview.md).

Recording audio is an explicitly temporary Audio cache, with an adjustable default retention of 24 hours. Transcript, explanations and personal annotations remain durable after audio expiry, and an imported Transcript does not require audio to be retained or downloaded. Recovering the current lecture from its folder does not imply recovering audio that the retention policy has already deleted. The interview records the retention clock's working interpretation and reference-informed recovery behavior.
